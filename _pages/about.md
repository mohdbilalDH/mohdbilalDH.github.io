I am a master's student in Korean Studies at [The Academy of Korean Studies](https://www.aks.ac.kr/), where I work on modern Korean history and its cultural afterlives. My research moves between the colonial period and the contemporary moment, and increasingly between archival and computational methods.

## Research interests

My work centres on three connected areas.

**Gender and the colonial archive.** I am interested in how women appear — and are made to appear — in the print culture of colonial Korea. Much of my current research concerns *kisaeng*, and how the periodical press of the 1920s and 1930s framed them as both objects of nostalgia and figures of modern anxiety. The sources are uneven, censored, and often hostile to the people they describe, which makes reading them a methodological problem as much as a historical one.

**Korean popular culture and its circulation.** I also work on the Korean Wave, particularly how Korean popular culture has been received and reframed in Indian media. This is a question about transnational cultural flows, but also about the vocabularies that receiving cultures reach for when describing something unfamiliar.

**Comparative cultural history.** I am drawn to comparisons between Korea and South Asia — colonial modernity, gendered performance traditions, and the uneven ways cultural forms travel between the two regions.

## Digital humanities

I am building a computational side to this work: corpus construction from Korean historical periodicals, text analysis of Hangul–Hanja mixed sources, historical GIS, and network analysis of the people and institutions that appear in the archive.

My interest in these methods is not in the tools themselves but in what they make visible. Colonial-era sources are fragmentary, censored, and full of uncertainty about dates, names, and authorship. Most computational work in the humanities smooths that uncertainty away. I would rather find ways to represent it — to build projects that show the limits of what the archive can support, alongside what it reveals.

Projects, datasets, and code will be published here as they develop, with openly licensed data and persistent identifiers wherever possible.
