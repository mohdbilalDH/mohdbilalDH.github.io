Editorial architecture

The magazine is not a flat list of articles. It contains prompted symposia, a framed round-table, serialised fiction that runs across both issues, recurring columns, and poems printed inside other people's articles. This is the structure the reconstruction recovered.

V2 · Editorial forms

Units are grouped by editorial form, nested items shown beneath their parent, and the two cross-issue serials linked.

Two units, used throughout this edition. An editorial unit is an item as the magazine itself presents it — one article, one column, or one prompt with its answers. A textual item is a single continuous text with its own title or byline: each answer to a prompt, and each poem printed inside someone else's article, is its own textual item. One editorial unit can therefore contain several textual items, which is why counts differ depending on which is being counted.

What this does not show. That the frequency of a form measures editorial intent or effort. That the round-table speakers 리 and 김 are identified people — only surname initials are printed. That "solicited" and "editor-written" are the same thing. Whether prompting shaped what contributors wrote is an open question this edition does not answer.
